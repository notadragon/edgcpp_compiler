## Rationale

P4283, "Requires-clauses on contract assertions" (M12): with
`--[no_]contracts_p4283` (implied by `--contracts_p3850` unless given, and
enabling contracts; an error in C; passed to g++ by the drivers as
`-f[no-]contracts-p4283`), and `__cpp_contracts_requires` 202609L, as our GCC
predefines it.

A precondition, postcondition or `contract_assert` of a templated function
may have a requires-clause, after its label and before its attributes and
captures.  Its tokens are cached from `requires` to the end of the
constraint (`cache_contract_requires_clause`), with GCC's boundary rule
(`cp_skip_contract_requires_clause`): the constraint ends before the last
parenthesized group ahead of the next specifier, a `,` after one, or the end
of the declaration or statement, and before the bracketed groups just ahead
of that operand (attributes, captures); a requires-expression's parameter
list and requirement-body belong to it.  The tokens after the constraint are
scanned again.  The clause is scanned with the operand
(`scan_cached_contract_requires_clause`, before the label), so it may name
the parameters, on every route: members and friends in the complete class,
lambdas in the call operator's body, templates per instance, postconditions
awaiting deduction.  The constraint is scanned as `scan_requires_clause`
does but kept on the assertion (`scan_contract_requires_constraint`, in
expr.c): no requires-clause entry is made, since those are in file-scope
memory and an assertion may be in a function's.  In a template-dependent
context it is recorded in `requires_ranges`, by the sequence number of its
`requires`, and as `a_contract_specifier::requires_constraint` (walked,
displayed, copied to file scope with the predicate, or reached through a
local expression node reference when it names a local entity).

An instance takes the constraint as written in its template (the
specifier's, or `requires_ranges`' when the template's tokens are scanned
again) and checks it with the arguments of the active instantiations
(`contract_requires_clause_satisfied`, as a requires-expression is checked):
a substitution failure in an atomic constraint makes it not satisfied, and a
settled conjunction or disjunction does not substitute its second operand.
An unsatisfied assertion is discarded: `instantiate_contract_specifiers`
makes no specifier for it, a specifier scanned in an instance (a lambda's, a
local class member's) is marked `discarded` and removed from the list
(`remove_discarded_contract_specifiers`), and a `contract_assert` becomes an
empty statement (`add_discarded_contract_assert_statement`), so that
constant evaluation and the C-generating back end's checks see only the
assertions kept.  Its label, captures and predicate are never scanned.

Redeclarations must have equivalent clauses (`compare_expressions`), or none
on both.  Without the option, and on an assertion that is not templated
where it is written (neither template-dependent nor in an instance), the
clause is diagnosed with GCC's text and the assertion dropped, as GCC does
(`skip_rest_of_contract_specifier`).

Templates are put out for g++ as written, clauses included, so g++ discards
the same assertions from its instances; a generic lambda put out from the IL
(in configurations that record its prototype instantiation) gets its
assertions' clauses (`gen_contract_requires_clause`).

The tests are `tests/tests/contracts/p4283/` and the imports in
`tests/tests/contracts/imported_p4283/`.
